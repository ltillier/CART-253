### Week 1:
I started this class with no clue about anything we were going to do and no idea what to expect. I had trouble setting up the necessary software and getting started, but I think I got the hang of things pretty quickly after asking (and getting!) help. I think the main thing that surprised me was how simple/intuitive the challenges/assignments were, since I was honestly anticipating that I would struggle a lot. A big part of that is probably due to the way the instructions are given (they're very easy to follow), but I'm also a little surprised at how much fun I had. I didn't do much in class, but as soon as I got home I began to fiddle around with my website. I think what I've struggled with the most so far has been asking for help and committing to do things even if I'm not sure if I'm doing them right but I'm not surprised since that's something I tend to worry about in general. I've been somewhat worried about my commit messages since a part of me wonders if I'm leaving too much information in the message portion (I tend to overuse parentheses and forward slashes when left to my own devices). I feel like there are a lot of directions I can go with this course (I also feel like I don't have a super clear idea of what I'm actually meant to end up with, in a way?). I want to be able to make something helpful for people but I also kind of want to make something that is somewhat odd, very useless, and fun.

### Week 2:
I missed class this week, which in hindsight, was a big miscalculation. As a result of missing class, I didn't work on the instructions challenge until Friday, which snowballed into issues with completing the prototypes in time for the next class (and also, in general). Through the process of making the prototypes, the main thing on my mind was that I just wasn't sure what I should actually aim to make. I like drawing because I've done it enough to be relatively good at doing what I want to do. I don't know what I'm doing, generally, when it comes to coding, and I kept running into issues where if I went in with a very clear idea of what I wanted to make, I would fail to meet my own expectations, and if I went in with no idea what to make, I would struggle to come up with a concrete plan, since the possibilities are endless and all equally unfamiliar to me. This isn't necessarily an issue in the grand scheme of things because I will get better at things. The biggest issue I had/am having is that I keep missing small (but vital!) spelling/grammar mistakes that result in nothing working for no good reason. After completing the instructions challenge (late), I struggled to set up the template files for prototypes, specifically because the createCanvas function was not working. I came to the next class with nothing, only to be told that the reason nothign worked was because I hadn't used pascalCase for the function. I should have spent more time trying to figure out why it wasn't working (at the same time, it's demoralizing when nothing works at all, and so I understand why I didn't keep trying when I had no idea what was going on). The other big issue I had is that I don't think anything I've made is very good - part of this is because I'm aware of the possibilities (beyond the scope of these specific prototypes) and so I keep thinking of things that aren't possible without variables/conditionals/etc. This is because I'm doing all this work late. I definitely think going forward I will try to finish the prototypes sooner rather than later. The other reason I think i dislike the prototypes I've made is generally because it's reading week and everything feels off. I've spent the last few days hemming and hawing over whether to redo or submit and the end result is that very little has gotten done.
